# Constitution

**Project:** Glyph Garden  
**Language:** Grammar of the Seasons  
**Document Version:** 0.1.0  
**Status:** Draft

---

## Purpose

The Constitution defines the enduring principles of Glyph Garden.

It does not prescribe specific geometry, modules, or glyphs. Instead, it establishes the values that guide every design decision made within the Grammar of the Seasons.

Where the Geometry Specification describes *how* glyphs are constructed, the Constitution explains *why* they are constructed that way.

These principles are intended to remain stable even as the grammar evolves.

---

# Principles

## 1. Geometry Comes First

Every glyph begins as geometry.

Illustration, symbolism, and ornament emerge from geometric relationships rather than being imposed upon them.

When forced to choose between decorative appeal and geometric clarity, geometric clarity takes precedence.

---

## 2. Growth Is Constructed

Glyphs are built through processes of growth.

Branches, rays, petals, and other forms should appear to emerge naturally from earlier structures.

Nothing should feel arbitrarily attached.

---

## 3. Simplicity Precedes Complexity

Complexity should arise through repetition, recursion, and transformation.

A sophisticated glyph should be recognizable as the natural consequence of a simple underlying system.

---

## 4. Every Line Earns Its Place

Each visible element must perform at least one meaningful function.

Functions may include:

- defining structure
- expressing hierarchy
- establishing rhythm
- indicating growth
- balancing composition

Decoration without purpose should be avoided.

---

## 5. Empty Space Is Structural

Negative space is part of the design.

It establishes rhythm, balance, and legibility.

Removing empty space is as significant a change as adding new geometry.

---

## 6. Consistency Creates Language

Individual glyphs are not independent illustrations.

They are members of the same visual language.

Shared construction methods are more valuable than isolated moments of visual cleverness.

---

## 7. One New Idea Per Plate

Construction Plates introduce exactly one significant concept.

New ideas should be isolated whenever possible.

This allows the language to evolve through small, understandable steps.

---

## 8. Discovery Over Invention

The project seeks to discover forms implied by the grammar rather than invent arbitrary ones.

Design should feel inevitable rather than surprising.

The strongest solutions often appear obvious in retrospect.

---

## 9. Constraints Enable Creativity

Constraints are not limitations.

They provide the structure from which meaningful variation emerges.

Whenever possible, new expressive possibilities should arise from existing rules rather than additional exceptions.

---

## 10. The Grammar Is Never Finished

Glyph Garden is intended to evolve.

New glyphs, modules, and construction methods may be added as the language grows.

However, additions should strengthen the grammar rather than complicate it.

Growth should deepen the system, not dilute it.

---

# Decision Making

When evaluating competing designs, preference should generally be given to the option that:

1. strengthens the grammar,
2. simplifies construction,
3. improves legibility,
4. reinforces consistency,
5. preserves opportunities for future growth.

---

# Scope

The Constitution is intentionally stable.

Changes should be rare and made only when experience demonstrates that one of its principles no longer serves the project.

Most future evolution should occur within the Geometry Specification, Construction Plates, and Pattern Library rather than within this document.

---

# Relationship to Other Documents

This document establishes the philosophical foundation of the project.

The remaining documentation derives from these principles.

- **Design Brief** defines the project's goals.
- **Geometry Specification** defines the construction rules.
- **Construction Plates** explore those rules incrementally.
- **Pattern Library** records successful reusable discoveries.
- **Design Decisions Log** documents important choices and their rationale.

---

> *A grammar is valuable not because it limits expression, but because it makes expression coherent.*
