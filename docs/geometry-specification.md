# Geometry Specification

**Project:** Glyph Garden  
**Language:** Grammar of the Seasons  
**Document Version:** 0.1.0  
**Status:** Draft

---

## Purpose

The Geometry Specification defines the structural vocabulary of the Grammar of the Seasons.

Unlike the Constitution, which establishes guiding principles, and the Design Brief, which defines project goals, this document describes the geometric concepts from which glyphs are constructed.

It intentionally describes *categories* of geometry rather than specific seasonal implementations.

Construction Plates, Module Sheets, and finished Glyphs provide the concrete realizations of this specification.

---

# Design Hierarchy

Glyph Garden is organized as a hierarchy of geometric ideas.

```
Universe
    ↓
Coordinate System
    ↓
Center
    ↓
Primary Structure
    ↓
Growth Stations
    ↓
Modules
    ↓
Assemblies
    ↓
Glyph
```

Each level depends upon the levels above it.

No level should require information from levels below it.

---

# Foundation

The following concepts are considered foundational.

They should change rarely.

## Construction Universe

Every glyph exists within a defined geometric universe.

The universe establishes:

- orientation
- scale
- rotational symmetry
- available construction space

The Construction Universe is introduced by Plate 000.

---

## Coordinate System

Every geometric element is positioned relative to a shared coordinate system.

The coordinate system should remain independent of any particular glyph.

Alternative coordinate systems may be explored experimentally but should not replace the canonical system without careful evaluation.

---

## Center

Every glyph possesses a single primary center.

The center serves as the origin of construction and establishes the visual balance of the glyph.

Multiple centers may exist only as deliberate higher-level structures.

---

# Primary Structure

Primary Structure establishes the first generation of geometry.

Examples include:

- stems
- axes
- rays
- spokes
- arms

Primary Structure provides the framework upon which later growth occurs.

---

# Growth

Glyphs develop through successive stages of growth.

Growth introduces new geometry by extending existing geometry.

New elements should emerge from established structures rather than appearing independently.

Growth may include:

- branching
- subdivision
- extension
- repetition
- rotation
- reflection

---

# Growth Stations

Growth Stations define locations where additional geometry may emerge.

Growth Stations establish potential rather than obligation.

Not every Growth Station must be used.

---

# Modules

Modules are reusable geometric constructions.

A module should represent a single coherent idea.

Examples might include:

- branch forms
- terminal motifs
- junctions
- petals
- diamonds
- rays

Modules are documented independently through Module Sheets.

---

# Assemblies

Assemblies combine multiple modules into larger structures.

Assemblies should preserve the identity of their constituent modules while producing new emergent forms.

Assemblies are expected to become increasingly sophisticated as the grammar develops.

---

# Symmetry

Symmetry is a structural property rather than an aesthetic requirement.

Possible forms include:

- rotational symmetry
- reflectional symmetry
- radial repetition

Individual glyphs determine which forms of symmetry they employ.

The specification does not prescribe a single symmetry order.

---

# Hierarchy

Geometry should communicate hierarchy visually.

Hierarchy may be expressed through:

- length
- spacing
- repetition
- branching depth
- structural dependency

Visual emphasis should emerge naturally from construction.

---

# Constraints

Constraints define the boundaries within which exploration occurs.

Constraints are intended to simplify decision making rather than limit creativity.

Whenever possible, new expressive possibilities should emerge from existing constraints.

---

# Parameters

The specification distinguishes between three kinds of parameters.

## Foundational Parameters

Stable characteristics shared by the entire language.

Examples include:

- coordinate system
- construction universe
- hierarchy
- growth model

---

## Seasonal Parameters

Characteristics that distinguish one glyph from another.

Examples may include:

- branching rhythm
- terminal style
- density
- expansion
- directional emphasis

---

## Exploratory Parameters

Experimental characteristics under investigation.

Successful explorations may eventually become Seasonal or Foundational parameters.

---

# Evolution

The Geometry Specification is expected to evolve.

However, additions should:

- simplify the language,
- improve consistency,
- increase expressive power,
- preserve compatibility whenever practical.

Growth through refinement is preferred over growth through accumulation.

---

# Relationship to Other Documents

The Geometry Specification describes the architecture of the language.

Its concepts are explored through:

- Construction Plates
- Module Sheets
- Pattern Library
- Design Decisions Log

Those documents define *how* individual ideas are implemented.

---
