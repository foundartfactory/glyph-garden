# Design Brief

**Project:** Glyph Garden  
**Language:** Grammar of the Seasons  
**Document Version:** 0.1.0  
**Status:** Draft

---

## Purpose

The Design Brief defines the scope, objectives, and intended character of Glyph Garden.

Unlike the Constitution, which establishes enduring principles, this document describes the project itself: what it aims to create, who it is for, and how success should be evaluated.

As the project evolves, the Design Brief may be refined without altering the underlying philosophy of the language.

---

# Project Vision

Glyph Garden is an open design language for creating seasonal glyphs through a shared geometric grammar.

Rather than producing a collection of unrelated illustrations, the project seeks to develop a coherent visual system whose forms emerge from common construction methods.

Every completed glyph should feel as though it belongs to the same family, regardless of the season or symbol it represents.

---

# Objectives

The project has five primary objectives.

## 1. Build a Grammar

Develop a geometric language capable of generating many related glyphs rather than a handful of isolated designs.

The grammar should be expressive enough to encourage exploration while remaining disciplined enough to maintain a recognizable identity.

---

## 2. Preserve the Design Process

Document not only finished glyphs, but also the reasoning, experiments, and construction methods that produced them.

The evolution of the language is considered part of the project's output.

---

## 3. Balance Structure and Expression

Maintain a clear distinction between structural geometry and expressive variation.

The underlying construction should remain consistent even as seasonal characteristics emerge.

---

## 4. Encourage Reuse

Successful geometric ideas should become reusable patterns.

The language should grow by recombining proven components rather than continually introducing new ones.

---

## 5. Invite Participation

Create documentation that allows others to understand, critique, and contribute to the language without requiring prior knowledge of its history.

The project should remain approachable while preserving its rigor.

---

# Initial Scope

Version 0.1 focuses on establishing the foundation of the language.

The initial glyph vocabulary consists of five seasonal symbols:

- Winter
- Spring
- Summer
- Autumn
- New Year

These glyphs are intended as demonstrations of the grammar rather than as a complete visual system.

---

# Non-Goals

Glyph Garden does **not** seek to:

- imitate botanical illustration,
- reproduce historical ornament,
- optimize for decorative complexity,
- create logos or branding assets,
- define a universal symbolic language.

The project may draw inspiration from many traditions, but it does not attempt to recreate any single aesthetic.

---

# Audience

Glyph Garden is intended for people who enjoy systems as much as artifacts.

Potential audiences include:

- designers,
- typographers,
- generative artists,
- SVG enthusiasts,
- creative coders,
- educators,
- mathematicians,
- makers,
- anyone curious about how visual languages are constructed.

No specialized mathematical background should be required to understand the documentation.

---

# Measures of Success

The project succeeds when:

- new glyphs can be constructed without inventing new geometry,
- different contributors produce recognizably related work,
- the documentation explains *why* designs look the way they do,
- experiments improve the grammar rather than merely expanding it,
- the language remains coherent as its vocabulary grows.

The project does **not** measure success by the number of glyphs produced.

---

# Design Character

Glyph Garden should feel:

- geometric,
- calm,
- intentional,
- balanced,
- discoverable,
- timeless,
- elegant without ornament,
- expressive through structure.

It should never feel:

- arbitrary,
- crowded,
- whimsical without purpose,
- visually noisy,
- overly literal,
- mechanically repetitive,
- dependent on decoration.

---

# Deliverables

The project currently produces four kinds of artifacts.

## Documentation

Specifications, philosophy, and historical decisions.

## Construction Plates

Incremental explorations introducing one new concept at a time.

## Module Sheets

Structured explorations of individual geometric components.

## Glyphs

Finished expressions of the grammar.

Each artifact contributes to understanding the language from a different perspective.

---

# Long-Term Vision

Over time, Glyph Garden may expand beyond the initial seasonal vocabulary.

Possible future directions include:

- astronomical glyphs,
- weather glyphs,
- botanical grammars,
- ceremonial symbols,
- procedural generation,
- educational materials,
- physical fabrication,
- software tools.

These expansions should emerge from the existing grammar rather than replacing it.
